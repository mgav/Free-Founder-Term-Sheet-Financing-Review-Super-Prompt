# Free Founder Term Sheet & Financing Review Super-Prompt

A free, founder-side AI prompt for reviewing venture financing term sheets, SAFEs, convertible notes, preferred stock financings, governance documents, cap tables, and related startup financing agreements.

Use it to help surface and explain:

- founder dilution;
- liquidation preferences;
- participating preferred;
- anti-dilution;
- option-pool effects;
- board and voting control;
- investor veto rights;
- drag-along provisions;
- founder vesting and re-vesting;
- future-round implications;
- down-round consequences;
- exit waterfalls;
- founder/common zero-proceeds thresholds;
- preference/conversion crossover points;
- investor/founder incentive conflicts;
- unusual or deal-specific definitions;
- and other provisions that may materially affect founders economically or through control.

> **Important:** This is an educational and issue-spotting tool. It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified attorney, tax advisor, accountant, or other professional.

## How to use

1. Copy the full prompt below.
2. Upload your term sheet and any related financing documents.
3. If available, also upload your current cap table.
4. Paste the prompt into a capable AI system.
5. Review the output carefully with qualified startup counsel before signing anything.

For the most useful analysis, provide as many of these as possible:

- term sheet;
- cap table;
- certificate of incorporation / charter;
- stock purchase agreement;
- investors' rights agreement;
- voting agreement;
- ROFR / co-sale agreement;
- side letters;
- SAFEs;
- convertible notes;
- warrants;
- founder stock agreements;
- equity plan;
- board consents;
- debt documents.

# FOUNDER DASHBOARD — SHOW THIS FIRST

After reviewing the uploaded documents sufficiently to understand the transaction, begin the substantive output with a **Founder Dashboard**.

The purpose is to let a founder understand the most consequential economics and control issues in approximately one minute before reading the detailed analysis.

Do not merely repeat headline terms from the term sheet.

Calculate the actual numbers wherever sufficient information exists.

Where a number cannot reliably be calculated, display **UNKNOWN** and identify exactly what document or information is missing.

Use a concise table or similarly clear format.

## CORE DASHBOARD

At minimum show:

### 1. Money being raised

- Gross financing amount
- Estimated transaction expenses
- Estimated net cash to company

### 2. Valuation

- Pre-money valuation
- Post-money valuation
- Price per share, if applicable

### 3. Founder ownership

Show:

- founder ownership before financing;
- founder ownership after option-pool expansion, if applicable;
- founder ownership immediately after financing.

### 4. Effective founder dilution

Do not merely show the investor's stated ownership percentage.

Include dilution caused by:

- new investment;
- option-pool increase;
- SAFE conversion;
- note conversion;
- accrued interest;
- warrants;
- other convertible or promised securities.

Show:

> **Headline dilution: ___%**

versus:

> **Effective founder dilution: ___%**

Explain the difference briefly.

### 5. Investor ownership

Show the new investor or investor group's ownership:

- on an issued/outstanding basis;
- on an as-converted basis;
- on a fully diluted basis, where relevant.

### 6. Aggregate liquidation preference

Show:

> **Total money preferred investors are entitled to receive before common participates, subject to the actual waterfall: $___**

Break this down by preferred series.

Clearly identify:

- preference multiple;
- participation;
- accrued dividends;
- seniority;
- pari passu treatment.

### 7. Founder/common zero-proceeds threshold

Calculate:

> **Approximate company sale price at or below which founders/common receive $0: $___**

If debt, fees, multiple preference classes, or other claims affect this threshold, incorporate them.

This is one of the most important dashboard numbers.

### 8. Founder/common first-dollar threshold

Calculate:

> **Approximate sale price at which founders/common receive their first dollar: $___**

If different from the zero-proceeds threshold because of waterfall mechanics, explain why.

### 9. Meaningful-founder-proceeds threshold

Also calculate, if feasible:

> **Approximate sale price at which founders begin receiving economically meaningful proceeds: $___**

Define what assumption you use for "meaningful" rather than pretending it is an objective legal threshold.

For example, show when founders receive:

- $100,000;
- $500,000;
- $1 million;

or another useful amount based on the deal size.

### 10. Preference/conversion crossover

Calculate:

> **Approximate exit value at which preferred investors are better off converting to common instead of taking their liquidation preference: $___**

If multiple preferred classes have different crossover points, show each separately.

### 11. Ownership-versus-economics comparison

Show something like:

> Founders own: **___%**

> Founders receive at a 0.5x exit: **___% of net equity proceeds**

> Founders receive at a 1x exit: **___%**

> Founders receive at a 5x exit: **___%**

Highlight situations where the founder's stated ownership percentage materially overstates the founder's actual share of exit proceeds.

### 12. Board control

Show:

- number of founder/common seats;
- investor seats;
- independent seats;
- total seats.

Then state:

> **Who controls the board today?**

and:

> **Can control change without founders voluntarily agreeing to it?**

Explain briefly.

### 13. Smallest ownership capable of blocking a major action

Determine, where possible:

> **Smallest investor or investor group that can block a material corporate action despite owning only ___% of the company.**

Identify which actions can be blocked.

Examples:

- new financing;
- sale of company;
- debt;
- board-size change;
- charter amendment;
- issuance of securities.

If one investor has an individual veto, say so prominently.

### 14. Sale-control threshold

Show:

> **Who can approve, block, or potentially force a sale of the company?**

Include the effect of:

- board approval;
- preferred votes;
- common votes;
- drag-along;
- protective provisions.

### 15. Next-round vulnerability

Give a concise answer:

> **If the next financing is a 50% down round, the three current provisions most likely to hurt founders are: ___, ___, and ___.**

### 16. Major-success persistence

Give a concise answer:

> **If the company becomes worth 10x–100x today's valuation, the current provisions that still matter most are: ___**

### 17. Most consequential economic provision

State:

> **Most consequential founder-economic term: ___**

Explain in one or two sentences.

### 18. Most consequential control provision

State:

> **Most consequential founder-control term: ___**

Explain briefly.

### 19. Most surprising term

State:

> **Term most likely to surprise an inexperienced founder: ___**

Explain why.

### 20. Biggest missing information

State:

> **The single most important missing fact/document preventing a reliable analysis is: ___**

# ANALYTICAL RULES

Throughout the review:

1. Show the math.
2. Define every specialized term.
3. Source every material definition.
4. Read contract definitions before applying customary meanings.
5. Flag nonstandard definitions.
6. Never equate valuation with founder economics.
7. Distinguish pre-money from post-money.
8. Distinguish authorized from issued shares.
9. Distinguish issued from fully diluted shares.
10. Distinguish preferred ownership from as-converted ownership.
11. Distinguish liquidation preference from creditor priority.
12. Distinguish gross sale price from net proceeds available to equity.
13. Account for multiple preferred series separately.
14. Account for dividends.
15. Account for SAFEs.
16. Account for notes.
17. Account for accrued interest.
18. Account for warrants.
19. Account for option pools.
20. State assumptions.
21. Do not invent cap-table information.
22. Flag undefined terms.
23. Flag ambiguous terms.
24. Cross-reference definitions.
25. Check formulas.
26. Look for drafting inconsistencies.
27. Look for document conflicts.
28. Look for side-letter rights.
29. Look for future-round effects.
30. Explain downside protection.
31. Explain upside participation.
32. Explain when each clause actually matters.
33. Pay special attention to low-probability/high-consequence clauses.
34. Identify terms whose significance changes from Seed to Series A/B/C/D.
35. Do not dismiss provisions as boilerplate without explaining them.
36. Always analyze exits below current valuation.
37. Always identify where founders/common receive zero.
38. Always identify preference/conversion crossover.
39. Always reconcile conflicting definitions.
40. Always analyze economics and control separately.
41. Always analyze positive and negative scenarios.
42. Always identify provisions that appear harmless alone but become consequential in combination.
43. Always distinguish legal rights from likely practical behavior.
44. Never assume the investor will act benevolently.
45. Never assume the investor will act maliciously.
46. Analyze what the documents legally permit and the incentives they create.
47. Never allow a headline ownership percentage to substitute for actual waterfall analysis.
48. Never allow a headline valuation to substitute for exit economics.
49. Put the most consequential founder information in the Founder Dashboard before presenting detailed analysis.
50. If the dashboard produces a surprising or counterintuitive result, explicitly explain why.

# BENCHMARKING

Where helpful, compare provisions with contemporary U.S. venture-financing practice and NVCA-style documents.

But:

- common does not mean founder-friendly;
- unusual does not automatically mean bad;
- identify stage-specific market practice;
- distinguish deal wording from NVCA wording;
- use current market information where available;
- disclose when current market data is needed.

---

# CENTRAL QUESTIONS

Throughout the review, continually ask:

> **How could this provision affect founders economically or in control of the company under circumstances that may not be obvious when signing today?**

> **If the company performs much worse than hoped, who gets the money first, how much do they get, and at what sale price do founders and employees begin receiving anything?**

> **If the company becomes enormously successful, which provisions still matter?**

> **Has the contract taken a familiar venture-financing term and defined it differently in a way that changes the outcome?**

> **What are the most economically impactful or control-impactful provisions for founders in both positive and negative scenarios?**

> **What is the realistic worst thing that could happen to founder economics or control under these documents?**

> **What are the handful of numbers a founder should know immediately — ownership, effective dilution, preference stack, zero-proceeds threshold, conversion crossover, and control/blocking thresholds?**

Those are the core purposes of this review.
